# V6.13-P1B2-R3 — FIXED_N Global governor audit (diagnostic only)

Tree: `5f11c7c` (R2 committed). **No file in `src/` or `tests/` was modified.** All measurements come from an external harness (monkeypatch of `_expandir_estado` and of the `global_rescue` call; scratchpad, not in the repo). Hardware: AC power for the whole campaign (`power.log` empty), no contention, single process.

Question: can Global of the FIXED_N deep path stop depending on its 180 s CPU deadline and end naturally or by a deterministic frontier, without runaway risk?

**Short answer: yes — class A (`NATURAL_TERMINATION_FEASIBLE`).** In every productive state known (BK52, BK53) Global ends `completo` by itself at 14–17 % of its structural work bound, its cost does not depend on what the upstream stages produced, and a work budget is not needed (and would be fragile: it has a one-expansion cliff, §6).

## 2. Expansions vs states generated (kept separate)

* **Expansion** = one call of `_expandir_estado` on one frontier state inside `_beam_search` (MRV SKU choice → multi-pallet candidates → top-K → child states). This is what `presupuesto_expansiones` counts (`MedidorTrabajo.registrar_nodos(1)` after each call; the limit is checked with strict `>` at the start of each depth and of each restart: a *soft cap at depth boundary*).
* **States generated** = Σ over expansions of the number of children returned (`stats["estados_generados"]`), counted *before* pruning and transposition filtering. It is a different quantity: 7.4 states per expansion in BK52, 9.3 in BK53, 6.6 in BK31-stress. I cross-checked my own sum against `stats["estados_generados"]`: identical in every run.

## 3. Work counter: deterministic, clock-independent, stable

* Identical across 3 production runs and 3 natural runs for each case (and across 4 different upstream states for BK52): 4 388 / 32 364 and 6 434 / 59 640. CPU varies ±3 % between runs; the counters do not vary at all.
* Independent of the clock: the natural runs ignore the deadline entirely and give the same counts as the production runs that ended before it.
* The budget mode agrees with the trace of expansions: caps 6 306 / 6 305 (BK53), 4 260 / 4 259 (BK52) and 3 000 (BK53) stopped at 6 434 / 6 306, 4 388 / 4 260 and 3 106 expansions respectively — each equal to the cumulative expansion count at a depth boundary read from the independent wrapper (e.g. 34 + 24·128 = 3 106).
* Final output in every production run: BK52 COMP `e028452d4bf44586` / LAYOUT `0c931402980db937` / attrs `2330d008f3d6553d`; BK53 COMP `f172759840c3a5e7` / LAYOUT `eba170225b45cfc4` / attrs `7dfe61240e231ea1` (N, boxes 114 / 208, 0 errors, source `DEEP_PATH_CAMINO_PROFUNDO`) — equal to the R2 gates.

## 4. Last useful work

Granularity: the best state is updated inside a depth, so "last improvement" is reported as the cumulative expansion count at the end of the depth where `cajas_colocadas_best`/debt last changed (the depth log is per depth).

| state | expansions to last improvement | expansions to completion / stagnation | margin | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P-BK52 | **4 388** (depth 37 of 38) | 4 388 | **0** | boxes by depth 9 → 114 monotone, 107 for 4 consecutive depths, then 110 / 112 / 114 |
| P-BK53 | **6 434** (depth 52 of 53) | 6 434 | **0** | 187 boxes from depth 39 to depth 46 (a **7-depth plateau ≈ 900 expansions with no improvement**), then 201 → 208 |
| S-BK31 restart 1 / 2 / 3 | 2 972 / 3 639 / 4 013 (cumulative) | 3 100 / 3 767 / 4 150 | 128 / 128 / 137 | stagnates after its last improvement; never reaches 100 % |

Consequences (no cap is proposed here): in both productive cases the *last* depth is the one that completes the CD, so any rule of the form "stop after k depths without improvement" or "stop at x % of the expected work" would cut the result (BK53 would stop in the plateau at 187/208). The minimum work for a complete result equals the work needed to *start* the last depth (§6).

## 5. Sensitivity to the input state

Global rebuilds the CD from empty pallets: it uses `pallets`/`sin_colocar` only to reconstruct the total demand (`_reconstruir_demanda_total`). The measurements confirm it is insensitive to the incumbent's quality:

| BK52 upstream state | missing at entry | Global expansions / states | stop | Global COMP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U1 RC2-like | 9 | 4 388 / 32 364 | `completo` | `e028452d4bf44586` |
| U2 Rescue 30 k | 1 | 4 388 / 32 364 | `completo` | `e028452d4bf44586` |
| U3 Rescue 56 k | 3 | 4 388 / 32 364 | `completo` | `e028452d4bf44586` |
| U4 Rescue natural | 3 | 4 388 / 32 364 | `completo` | `e028452d4bf44586` |
| P-BK52 (production) | 3 | 4 388 / 32 364 | `completo` | `e028452d4bf44586` |

Global's cost is a function of the CD (demand, pallets objetivo, candidate sets), not of the upstream incumbent. The only influence of upstream on the final output is the pipeline's accept rule (Global's result replaces the incumbent only if its score is strictly better). BK31 is the case where Global does *not* complete (stress state: `estancado`, 4 → 15 missing, rejected): there the final output depends on Rescue solving, as already established in P1B1.1.

## 6. Natural completion and the budget cliff

Smallest expansion cap that still lets Global finish `completo`, found by stepping ±1 around the work needed to start the last depth:

| case | cap | stop | expansions | boxes missing | Global COMP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BK53 | 6 306 | `completo` | 6 434 | 0 | `f172759840c3a5e7` |
| BK53 | 6 305 | `timeout` | 6 306 | 1 | `37a468a7c144130d` |
| BK52 | 4 260 | `completo` | 4 388 | 0 | `e028452d4bf44586` |
| BK52 | 4 259 | `timeout` | 4 260 | 2 | `f3738f60feed1525` |
| BK53 | 3 000 | `timeout` | 3 106 | 52 | `e5d1e18d750df277` |

A budget one expansion short gives a different, worse CD. The threshold (`B_min` = 6 306 / 4 260) is 98 % / 97 % of the natural work, and it is not a function of any simple input (BK52 4 260 expansions for 114 boxes ≈ 37 per box; BK53 6 306 for 208 boxes ≈ 30 per box). A fixed budget calibrated on these two cases would be fragile and would repeat the B2 mistake (one number tuned on a case breaking another). This is the same finding as for Rescue in P1B1.1: no early plateau exists, the work has to be done in full.

**Runaway bound.** Each depth runs at most `beam_width` expansions (observed: exactly 128 in 50 of the 53 BK53 depths; the other three are the first depths, where the frontier is still small; BK52 has 4 shorter depths near the end), the three restarts have beams 128 / 64 / 32, and a depth that places nothing ends the restart (`estancado`). With depth ≤ demand boxes, the work is bounded by `(128+64+32) · demanda_cajas`:

| case | structural bound (expansions) | natural | natural / bound | bound in CPU at observed ms/expansion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BK52 | 25 536 | 4 388 | 17 % | ≈ 590 s |
| BK53 | 46 592 | 6 434 | 14 % | ≈ 650 s |

(The bound relies on each continuing depth adding ≥ 1 box; I read it from the code and it holds in all observed logs, but I did not prove it formally. Deep-path CDs are small — N = 1–2 — so the bound is minutes, not hours.)

## 7. Evidence of the failure under throttling (not repeated on purpose)

Registered as `ENVIRONMENT_INVALIDATED_RUN` (R1 report §5, accidental battery-power runs): the 180 s CPU deadline cut Global before it finished and changed the output.

| run | Global states reached | natural | stop | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BK53 run 2 | 58 360 | 59 640 (97.9 %) | `timeout` | 207/208, COMP `dab4ea0c72b785c3` |
| BK53 run 3 | 53 449 | 59 640 (89.6 %) | `timeout` | 207/208, same COMP |
| BK52 run 3 | 30 932 | 32 364 (95.6 %) | `timeout` | 111/114 (Rescue watchdog also fired in that run) |

On a quiet machine Global needs 84–100 s CPU: the deadline (180 s) leaves only **1.8× (BK52) – 2.0× (BK53)** of margin. Under throttling the same expansions consume more CPU seconds (`process_time` counts consumed CPU time), so a ≈ 2× slowdown is enough to change a delivered result. This is the measured dependence on hardware.

Other fact worth recording: the CPU split of the full pipeline (3 runs each, AC power) is BK52 ≈ 75 s before the cascade + Rescue ≈ 86 s + Repair/Exact ≈ 112 s + Global ≈ 97–100 s; BK53 ≈ 171 s before the cascade + Rescue/Repair/Exact ≈ 24 s + Global ≈ 84–90 s. The R2 report paragraph that said BK53 is dominated by Global was wrong and is corrected there. The ≈ 171 s (BK53) and ≈ 75 s (BK52) before the cascade have not been attributed to a stage.

## 8. Proposal for R4 (not implemented)

Mirror R1 for Global, only on the FIXED_N deep call (`gobernador_profundo=True`):

1. `global_rescue(..., watchdog_cpu_segundos=W)`: `reloj` = `RelojTrabajoConWatchdog` (counts expansions), `limite_tiempo = SIN_TOPE`, so the checks at the start of each depth/restart never fire; the stage ends by `completo` / `estancado` (and the multi-start continues only while it has not completed). The 180 s `deadline` is ignored in this mode, as for Rescue.
2. Watchdog = safety only. Suggest `W ≥ 1 800 s CPU` (≥ 2.7× the structural bound in CPU above, ≈ 18× the natural CPU). Optionally also a deterministic **structural** expansion cap `224 · demanda_cajas` (derived from the algorithm, not calibrated on a case) so that the guard does not depend on the machine at all. If either fires → `watchdog_triggered` in the `fixed_n_deep_governor` metadata, WARNING, gate FAIL (as in R1); never silently returned as equivalent.
3. No fixed budget, no per-CD logic. Repair and Exact stay as they are (they do not change the final output in the known population because Global rebuilds the CD).
4. Not combined with the probe/final budgets (`presupuesto_*` keeps priority), so OPEN and the final materialization are unchanged.
5. Gates: BK52 / BK53 ×3 (expansions 4 388 / 6 434, states 32 364 / 59 640, `completo`, identical COMP/LAYOUT/attrs), BK31 (Global not reached), Golden 6, the 4 BK31 tests, the R1/R2 test file + new Global-governor tests (stop reasons, watchdog metadata, wiring only changes Global, mutation checks).
6. Open question for R4: Global CPU (≈ 23 ms / expansion in BK52) becomes the dominant cost once Rescue is P1-A-accelerated; a P1-A-style speed-up of `_expandir_estado` is a separate, later step and must not change expansion counts.

Risks to state: (a) the evidence is two productive cases (BK52, BK53) plus a stress case; (b) a CD with a much larger demand would take proportionally longer than 180 s before it completes — that is the intended trade (determinism over wall-time), but it must be accepted explicitly; (c) the structural bound is argued from the code, not proven.

## 9. Classification

* **A. `NATURAL_TERMINATION_FEASIBLE`** — for every productive state known (BK52 ×10 runs/states, BK53 ×6 runs): `completo`, 14–17 % of the structural bound, no runaway, deterministic, independent of the upstream incumbent.
* Non-productive state (S-BK31, `SHADOW_STRESS_ONLY`): also terminates naturally (`estancado` in all 3 restarts, 4 150 expansions, 25 s); it does not complete, which is a property of that state, not a governor problem.
* **B. `DETERMINISTIC_EXPANSION_BUDGET_REQUIRED`: not supported by the evidence** — the only deterministic budgets that preserve the result are ≥ `B_min` (one expansion short already changes the CD), i.e. effectively the natural work.
* C. `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` applies only to the *generalisation* (2 productive cases); it is covered by the watchdog / structural cap in the R4 proposal.
